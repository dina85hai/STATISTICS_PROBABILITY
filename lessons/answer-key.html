Question 4: Gaming Loot Box 🎮

A loot box contains: 50 common items, 30 rare items, 15 epic items, and 5 legendary items.

Are these events mutually exclusive or independent? Explain.

  • Event A: Getting a rare item
  • Event B: Getting an epic item
ANSWER: Mutually Exclusive
EXPLANATION: When you open ONE loot box, you get exactly ONE item. You cannot get both a rare item AND an epic item from the same box at the same time. Since these events cannot happen simultaneously, they are mutually exclusive.

Key Points to Award Credit:
  • ✓ Identifies as mutually exclusive
  • ✓ Explains you get only one item per box
  • ✓ States they cannot both happen at the same time
Common Student Error: Some may say "independent" - these are NOT independent because we're talking about the same loot box opening, not two separate events.

Question 5: Food Delivery 🍕

A food delivery app shows 12 pizza restaurants, 8 burger places, 6 sushi spots, and 4 taco trucks nearby.

What's the probability of randomly selecting a sushi spot?

WORK: Total restaurants = 12 + 8 + 6 + 4 = 30 Favorable outcomes (sushi) = 6 P(sushi) = Favorable/Total = 6/30 = 1/5
ANSWER: 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%
(Accept any equivalent form)

Question 6: Mystery Box 📦

A mystery box contains 15 items: 5 tech gadgets, 7 clothing items, and 3 gift cards.

What's the probability of NOT getting a tech gadget?

WORK (Method 1 - Direct): Total items = 15 Not tech = clothing + gift cards = 7 + 3 = 10 P(not tech) = 10/15 = 2/3 WORK (Method 2 - Complement): P(tech) = 5/15 = 1/3 P(not tech) = 1 - 1/3 = 2/3
ANSWER: 2/3 ≈ 0.667 ≈ 66.7%
(Accept any equivalent form; both methods valid)

A limited sneaker drop has 500 pairs available. 200,000 people enter the raffle.

What's your probability of winning?

WORK: Favorable outcomes (winning) = 500 pairs Total entries = 200,000 P(winning) = 500/200,000 = 1/400
ANSWER: 1/400 = 0.0025 = 0.25%
(Accept any equivalent form)
This is a very low probability - only 1 in 400 chance, showing how competitive limited drops are!

SECTION 4: INDEPENDENT EVENTS (4 points)

Key Formula for This Section:
P(A AND B) = P(A) × P(B)
Use multiplication rule when events are independent!
1 pt
Question 14: Two Spinners 🎡

You have two spinners:

  • Spinner 1: 4 equal sections (Red, Blue, Green, Yellow)
  • Spinner 2: 2 equal sections (Win, Lose)

What's the probability of spinning Blue AND Win?

WORK: These are independent events (spinners don't affect each other) Use: P(A AND B) = P(A) × P(B) P(Blue on Spinner 1) = 1/4 P(Win on Spinner 2) = 1/2 P(Blue AND Win) = 1/4 × 1/2 = 1/8
ANSWER: 1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5%
1 pt
Question 15: Social Media Posts 📸

When you post on Instagram, there's a 30% chance your crush will like it. When you post on TikTok, there's a 40% chance they'll view it.

If you post on BOTH platforms today, what's the probability your crush will like your Instagram post AND view your TikTok?

WORK: These are independent events (IG doesn't affect TikTok) Use: P(A AND B) = P(A) × P(B) P(Like on IG) = 30% = 0.30 P(View on TikTok) = 40% = 0.40 P(Like AND View) = 0.30 × 0.40 = 0.12
ANSWER: 0.12 = 12%
1 pt
Question 16: Free Throws 🏀

A basketball player makes 75% of their free throws. They get to shoot 3 free throws.

What's the probability they make ALL THREE shots?

WORK: Each shot is independent Use: P(A AND B AND C) = P(A) × P(B) × P(C) P(Make shot 1) = 0.75 P(Make shot 2) = 0.75 P(Make shot 3) = 0.75 P(Make all 3) = 0.75 × 0.75 × 0.75 = 0.421875
ANSWER: 0.421875 ≈ 0.422 ≈ 42.2%
(Accept 0.42 or 42% with rounding)
Interesting: Even a 75% shooter only makes all three about 42% of the time!

Question 17: Online Shopping 🛒

You order from two different websites:

  • Site A: 90% chance your order arrives on time
  • Site B: 85% chance your order arrives on time

What's the probability BOTH orders arrive on time?

WORK: The deliveries are independent events Use: P(A AND B) = P(A) × P(B) P(A on time) = 0.90 P(B on time) = 0.85 P(Both on time) = 0.90 × 0.85 = 0.765
ANSWER: 0.765 = 76.5%

SECTION 5: MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE EVENTS (3 points)

Key Formula for This Section:
P(A OR B) = P(A) + P(B)
Use addition rule when events are mutually exclusive!
1 pt
Question 18: Grade Distribution 📝

In your class, the grade distribution is:

  • A: 15% of students
  • B: 30% of students
  • C: 35% of students
  • D: 15% of students
  • F: 5% of students

What's the probability a randomly selected student has a passing grade (A, B, or C)?

WORK: These grades are mutually exclusive (can't get two grades) Use: P(A OR B OR C) = P(A) + P(B) + P(C) P(Passing) = P(A) + P(B) + P(C) P(Passing) = 0.15 + 0.30 + 0.35 = 0.80
ANSWER: 0.80 = 80%
1 pt
Question 19: Concert Raffle 🎫

A radio station is giving away concert tickets. They'll randomly select:

  • 1st prize: Front row seats (5 winners)
  • 2nd prize: Regular seats (15 winners)
  • 3rd prize: Meet & greet pass (10 winners)

You entered once. There are 1,000 total entries.

What's the probability you win ANY prize?

WORK (Method 1): These prizes are mutually exclusive (only win one) Use: P(1st OR 2nd OR 3rd) = P(1st) + P(2nd) + P(3rd) P(1st) = 5/1000 = 0.005 P(2nd) = 15/1000 = 0.015 P(3rd) = 10/1000 = 0.010 P(Any prize) = 0.005 + 0.015 + 0.010 = 0.030 WORK (Method 2 - Shortcut): Total prizes = 5 + 15 + 10 = 30 P(Any prize) = 30/1000 = 0.030
ANSWER: 0.030 = 3%
(Also accept 30/1000 or 3/100)

Question 20: Video Game Drops 🎮

In a video game, when you defeat a boss:

  • 10% chance: Legendary weapon drops
  • 25% chance: Epic armor drops
  • 30% chance: Rare accessory drops
  • 35% chance: Common item drops

These are mutually exclusive (you get exactly ONE reward per boss).

Part A: What's the probability you get a legendary weapon OR epic armor?
WORK: Mutually exclusive - use addition P(Legendary OR Epic) = P(L) + P(E) P(L or E) = 0.10 + 0.25 = 0.35
ANSWER (0.33 pt): 0.35 = 35%
Part B: What's the probability you get at least a rare item (rare, epic, or legendary)?
WORK: P(Rare OR Epic OR Legendary) = P(R) + P(E) + P(L) P(R or E or L) = 0.30 + 0.25 + 0.10 = 0.65
ANSWER (0.33 pt): 0.65 = 65%
Part C: Are you more likely to get something rare+ or something common?
WORK: P(Common) = 35% P(Rare or better) = 65% (from Part B) Compare: 65% > 35%
ANSWER (0.34 pt): More likely to get rare or better (65% vs 35%)
OR: "Rare+" / "Something rare or better"
