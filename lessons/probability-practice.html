1 4.3.1 Vocabulary: experiment, outcome, sample space and event

Word box

Example: you flip a coin once.

Experimentthe action you doflip a coin
OutcomeONE resultHeads
Sample space (S)ALL the results, in { }S = {Heads, Tails}
Eventthe result(s) you are looking forgetting Heads

n(S) means "how many results are in S". Here n(S) = 2.

Q1 · Flip a coin 🪙

Easy

Fill in each blank with a word from the word box: experiment, outcome or sample space.

  1. Flipping the coin is the ____________.
  2. Getting Tails is one ____________.
  3. {Heads, Tails} is the ____________.
Answer
  1. experiment (it is the action you do)
  2. outcome (it is one result)
  3. sample space (it lists all the results)

Q2 · Roll a die 🎲

Easy

You roll a normal die once. It shows 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6.

  1. Write the sample space S.
  2. What is n(S)?
  3. Event E = "get an even number". List the outcomes in E.
  4. What is n(E)?
Answer
  1. S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}
  2. n(S) = 6 (count the numbers in S)
  3. E = {2, 4, 6}
  4. n(E) = 3

Q3 · Canteen spinner 🍛

Medium

The school canteen spins a wheel to pick today's special. The wheel has 4 equal parts: Nasi lemak, Mee goreng, Roti canai and Laksa.

  1. Write the sample space S. What is n(S)?
  2. Event N = "the special is a noodle dish". List the outcomes in N. What is n(N)?
  3. Is "the wheel lands on Roti canai" one outcome or an event with more than one outcome?
Answer
  1. S = {Nasi lemak, Mee goreng, Roti canai, Laksa}, n(S) = 4
  2. N = {Mee goreng, Laksa}, n(N) = 2 (both are noodles)
  3. One outcome: the wheel lands on only one food.

2 4.3.1 Basic concept of probability

Formula

P(A) = n(A)/n(S) = number of favorable outcomestotal number of outcomes

P(not A) = 1 − P(A)

Always simplify the fraction. You can also give it as a decimal or a percentage. In syllabus language: calculate the probability of an event by dividing the number of outcomes in the event by the number of outcomes in the sample space.

Q4 · Food delivery 🍕

Easy

A food delivery app shows 12 pizza restaurants, 8 burger places, 6 sushi spots and 4 taco trucks nearby.

What is the probability of randomly selecting a sushi spot?

Answer
Total restaurants: n(S) = 12 + 8 + 6 + 4 = 30 Sushi spots: n(sushi) = 6 P(sushi) = 6/30 = 1/5

1/5 = 0.2 = 20%

Q5 · Class presentation 📊

Easy

Your teacher picks one student at random to present. The class has 8 students who finished their project and 4 who didn't. You finished yours.

Part A: What is the probability that you are picked?

Answer
Total students: 8 + 4 = 12. You are 1 of them. P(you) = 1/12

1/12 ≈ 0.083 ≈ 8.3%

Part B: What is the probability that someone who finished is picked?

Answer
Students who finished: 8 out of 12 P(finished) = 8/12 = 2/3

2/3 ≈ 0.667 ≈ 66.7%

Q6 · Streaming 📺

Medium

Your "Continue Watching" list has 6 shows you're bored of, 3 shows you love and 1 show you haven't started yet.

If you click one at random, what is the probability you pick something good (a show you love or the new one)?

Answer
Total shows: 6 + 3 + 1 = 10 Good shows: 3 + 1 = 4 P(good) = 4/10 = 2/5

2/5 = 0.4 = 40%

Q7 · Mystery box 📦

Medium

A mystery box contains 15 items: 5 tech gadgets, 7 clothing items and 3 gift cards.

What is the probability of NOT getting a tech gadget?

Answer
Method 1 (count): not tech = 7 + 3 = 10 items P(not tech) = 10/15 = 2/3 Method 2 (complement): P(tech) = 5/15 = 1/3 P(not tech) = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3

2/3 ≈ 0.667 ≈ 66.7%

Q8 · Sneaker drop 👟

Medium

A limited sneaker drop has 500 pairs. 200,000 people enter the raffle, one entry each.

What is your probability of winning a pair?

Answer
Winning entries: 500. Total entries: 200,000 P(win) = 500/200,000 = 1/400

1/400 = 0.0025 = 0.25%

That is a 1 in 400 chance, which is why limited drops are so hard to win.

Q9 · Fast food order 🍔

Easy

  • Side: Fries (F) or Salad (Sa)

Part A: Draw a tree diagram and list all the meals.

Answer

4 meals: SF, SSa, LF, LSa (2 × 2 = 4)

Part B: What is the probability of getting a large meal?

Answer
Large meals: LF, LSa = 2 out of 4 P(large) = 2/4 = 1/2

1/2 = 0.5 = 50%

Part C: What is the probability of getting fries?

Answer
Meals with fries: SF, LF = 2 out of 4 P(fries) = 2/4 = 1/2

1/2 = 0.5 = 50%

Q10 · Phone customization 📱

  • Case colour: Black (B), Clear (C) or Pink (P)

Q12 · Weekend plans 🎉

Hard
  • Morning: Gym (G) or Sleep in (S)
  • Afternoon: Movie (M) or Mall (Ma)
  • Evening: Party (P) or Chill (C)

Each choice is equally likely.

Part A: How many different ways can your day go?

Answer
2 × 2 × 2 = 8 GMP, GMC, GMaP, GMaC, SMP, SMC, SMaP, SMaC

8 ways

Part B: What is the probability your day includes the gym AND the party?

Answer
Gym and party: GMP, GMaP = 2 out of 8 P(gym and party) = 2/8 = 1/4

1/4 = 0.25 = 25%

Part C: What is the probability you sleep in OR chill in the evening (or both)?

Answer
Sleep in: SMP, SMC, SMaP, SMaC (4) Chill: GMC, GMaC, SMC, SMaC (4) Both: SMC, SMaC (2), so count them only once Sleep in or chill: SMP, SMC, SMaP, SMaC, GMC, GMaC = 6 out of 8 P(sleep in or chill) = 6/8 = 3/4

3/4 = 0.75 = 75%

Check with the overlap rule: 4/8 + 4/8 − 2/8 = 6/8. Adding 4 + 4 = 8 would count SMC and SMaC twice.

4 4.3.2 Dependent events (AND, without replacement)

Formula

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B | A)

Events are dependent when the first one changes the probability of the second, for example when you pick without replacement. P(B | A) means "the probability of B, given that A has already happened": after the first pick, the total and the count both go down.

Q17 · Dependent or independent? 🤔

Easy

Ask: does the first event change the numbers for the second? Decide for each one.

  1. Roll a die twice.
  2. Pick 2 prefects from 25 students.
  3. Take a pen from a box, put it back, then take another pen.
  4. Eat a chocolate from a box, then take another chocolate.
Answer
a) Independent: the die is the same every roll. b) Dependent: the same student can't be picked twice, so 24 are left. c) Independent: the pen goes back, so the box is the same again. d) Dependent: the chocolate is eaten, so one fewer is left.

a) Independent · b) Dependent · c) Independent · d) Dependent

Tip: "put back" → independent. "keep", "eat" or "pick different people" → dependent.

Q18 · Duit raya packets 🧧

Easy

A bag has 10 duit raya packets: 3 contain RM10 and 7 contain RM5. You take one and keep it, then your cousin takes one.

What is the probability that you BOTH get RM10?

Answer
P(you get RM10) = 3/10 You keep it, so 2 RM10 packets are left out of 9. P(cousin gets RM10 | you got RM10) = 2/9 P(both RM10) = 3/10 × 2/9 = 6/90 = 1/15

1/15 ≈ 0.067 ≈ 6.7%

Q19 · Faulty fuses 🔌

Easy

A box has 10 fuses and 3 of them are faulty. A technician tests 2 fuses, one after the other, and does not put the first one back.

Part A: What is the probability that BOTH fuses are faulty?

Answer
P(1st faulty) = 3/10 Now 2 faulty are left out of 9. P(2nd faulty | 1st faulty) = 2/9 P(both faulty) = 3/10 × 2/9 = 6/90 = 1/15

1/15 ≈ 0.067 ≈ 6.7%

Part B: What is the probability that the first is good and the second is faulty?

Answer
P(1st good) = 7/10 All 3 faulty fuses are still there, out of 9. P(2nd faulty | 1st good) = 3/9 P(good then faulty) = 7/10 × 3/9 = 21/90 = 7/30

7/30 ≈ 0.233 ≈ 23.3%

Q20 · Class committee 🗳️

Medium

A class has 30 students: 12 girls and 18 boys. The teacher picks a chairperson at random, then a secretary at random from the students who are left.

What is the probability that both are girls?

Answer
The same student can't have both jobs, so the events are dependent. P(chair is a girl) = 12/30 P(secretary is a girl | chair is a girl) = 11/29 P(both girls) = 12/30 × 11/29 = 132/870 = 22/145

22/145 ≈ 0.152 ≈ 15.2%

Q21 · Drink sachets ☕

Medium

A box has 5 Milo sachets and 3 teh tarik sachets. You take 2 sachets at random.

Part A: You put the first sachet back before taking the second. What is P(both Milo)?

Answer
With replacement the box is the same both times, so the events are independent. P(both Milo) = 5/8 × 5/8 = 25/64

25/64 ≈ 0.391 ≈ 39.1%

Part B: You keep the first sachet. What is P(both Milo)?

Answer
Without replacement: 4 Milo left out of 7. P(both Milo) = 5/8 × 4/7 = 20/56 = 5/14

5/14 ≈ 0.357 ≈ 35.7%

It is smaller than Part A because taking a Milo first leaves fewer Milo sachets for the second pick.

Part C: Without replacement, what is P(one Milo and one teh tarik, in any order)?

Answer
Milo then teh tarik: 5/8 × 3/7 = 15/56 Teh tarik then Milo: 3/8 × 5/7 = 15/56 The two orders can't both happen, so add them: 15/56 + 15/56 = 30/56 = 15/28

15/28 ≈ 0.536 ≈ 53.6%

Q22 · Two hearts 🃏

Medium

You draw 2 cards from a normal deck of 52 cards (13 hearts).

Part A: You keep the first card. What is P(both hearts)?

Answer
Without replacement: 12 hearts left out of 51. P(both hearts) = 13/52 × 12/51 = 156/2652 = 1/17

1/17 ≈ 0.059 ≈ 5.9%

Part B: You put the first card back and shuffle. What is P(both hearts)?

Answer
With replacement the deck is the same, so the events are independent. P(both hearts) = 13/52 × 13/52 = 1/4 × 1/4 = 1/16

1/16 = 0.0625 = 6.25%

Keeping the first heart leaves fewer hearts, so the dependent answer (5.9%) is a little smaller.

Q23 · Blind-box figures 🎁

Hard

A shelf has 12 blind boxes and 4 of them hold a rare figure. You buy 3 different boxes at random.

Part A: What is the probability that NONE of your 3 boxes is rare?

Answer
Not rare: 8 of 12, then 7 of 11, then 6 of 10. P(no rare) = 8/12 × 7/11 × 6/10 = 336/1320 = 14/55

14/55 ≈ 0.255 ≈ 25.5%

Part B: What is the probability that you get AT LEAST ONE rare figure?

Answer
"At least one rare" is the complement of "no rare". P(at least one rare) = 1 − 14/55 = 41/55

41/55 ≈ 0.745 ≈ 74.5%

5 4.3.3 Independent events (AND → multiply)

Formula

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)

Use it only when one event does not change the probability of the other.

Q13 · Two spinners 🎡

Easy
  • Spinner 1: 4 equal sections (Red, Blue, Green, Yellow)
  • Spinner 2: 2 equal sections (Win, Lose)

What is the probability of spinning Blue AND Win?

Answer
The spinners don't affect each other, so the events are independent. P(Blue) = 1/4, P(Win) = 1/2 P(Blue and Win) = 1/4 × 1/2 = 1/8

1/8 = 0.125 = 12.5%

Q14 · Social media posts 📸

Medium

When you post on Instagram, there is a 30% chance your crush likes it. When you post on TikTok, there is a 40% chance they view it. The two are independent.

You post on both today. What is the probability they like your Instagram post AND view your TikTok?

Answer
P(like) = 0.30, P(view) = 0.40 P(like and view) = 0.30 × 0.40 = 0.12

0.12 = 12%

Q15 · Online shopping 🛒

Medium

You order from two different websites. Site A delivers on time 90% of the time, and Site B 85% of the time.

What is the probability BOTH orders arrive on time?

Answer
The two deliveries are independent. P(both on time) = 0.90 × 0.85 = 0.765

0.765 = 76.5%

Q16 · Free throws 🏀

Hard

A basketball player makes 75% of their free throws. They shoot 3 free throws, and each shot is independent.

What is the probability they make ALL THREE?

Answer
P(make all 3) = P(1st) × P(2nd) × P(3rd) = 0.75 × 0.75 × 0.75 = 0.421875

0.421875 ≈ 0.422 ≈ 42.2%

Even a 75% shooter makes all three only about 42% of the time.

6 4.3.4 Mutually exclusive and 4.3.5 non-mutually exclusive events

Formula

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)

Use it only when A and B cannot happen at the same time. If they can overlap, they are non-mutually exclusive, so use P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B).

Q24 · Gaming loot box 🎮

Easy

A loot box contains 50 common, 30 rare, 15 epic and 5 legendary items. You open one box and get one item.

Are these events mutually exclusive or independent? Explain.

  • Event A: getting a rare item
  • Event B: getting an epic item
Answer

Mutually exclusive

One box gives exactly one item, so you cannot get a rare item AND an epic item from the same box. They are not independent: they are two results of the same box, not two separate experiments.

Q25 · Grade distribution 📝

Medium

In your class: A 15%, B 30%, C 35%, D 15%, F 5%.

What is the probability a randomly selected student has a passing grade (A, B or C)?

Answer
A student has only one grade, so the grades are mutually exclusive. P(A or B or C) = 0.15 + 0.30 + 0.35 = 0.80

0.80 = 80%

Q26 · Concert raffle 🎫

Medium

A radio station gives away: 1st prize to 5 winners, 2nd prize to 15 winners and 3rd prize to 10 winners. Each entry can win only one prize. There are 1,000 entries and you entered once.

What is the probability you win ANY prize?

Answer
P(1st) = 5/1000, P(2nd) = 15/1000, P(3rd) = 10/1000 P(any prize) = 5/1000 + 15/1000 + 10/1000 = 30/1000 = 3/100

3/100 = 0.03 = 3%

Q27 · Video game drops 🗡️

Hard

When you defeat a boss you get exactly ONE reward: legendary weapon 10%, epic armour 25%, rare accessory 30%, common item 35%.

Part A: What is the probability of a legendary weapon OR epic armour?

Answer
P(legendary or epic) = 0.10 + 0.25 = 0.35

0.35 = 35%

Part B: What is the probability of at least a rare item (rare, epic or legendary)?

Answer
P(rare or epic or legendary) = 0.30 + 0.25 + 0.10 = 0.65 Check: 1 − P(common) = 1 − 0.35 = 0.65

0.65 = 65%

Part C: Are you more likely to get something rare or better, or something common?

Answer

Rare or better (65% is more than 35%)

7 Probability trees: put it all together

Key idea

Multiply along a path to get the probability of that outcome (AND).

Add between paths when the question allows several outcomes (OR). All the outcomes together add up to 1.
