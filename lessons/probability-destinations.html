4.3.1 The basic concept of probability LO 8

🏝️ Destination: Langkawi, Kedah

Formula

P(A) = n(A)/n(S) = number of outcomes in event Anumber of outcomes in the sample space

0 ≤ P(A) ≤ 1     P(A′) = 1 − P(A)

P = 0 means impossible, P = 1 means certain. A′ ("A complement") means "A does not happen". To calculate the probability of an event, count the favorable outcomes in the event and divide by the total outcomes in the sample space.

Key words, Langkawi style

Experiment
Drawing one activity ticket from the hotel's lucky jar.
Outcome
One result, e.g. the ticket says "Cable car".
Sample space, S
All 12 tickets in the jar, so n(S) = 12.
Event
A group of outcomes, e.g. "a water activity".

4.3.2 Dependent events LO 9

🍓 Destination: Cameron Highlands, Pahang

Formula

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B | A)

Two events are dependent when the first one changes the probability of the second. This happens when you pick without replacement. P(B | A) means "the probability of B, given that A has already happened".

🧺 Interactive: the strawberry farm basket

At a strawberry farm you pick 2 fruits from a basket of 5 ripe 🍓 and 3 unripe 🟢 strawberries, without looking.

Tree of all the possibilities

Quick check: without replacement, what is P(one ripe and one unripe, in any order)?
P(ripe then unripe) = 5/8 × 3/7 = 15/56 P(unripe then ripe) = 3/8 × 5/7 = 15/56 These two paths can't both happen, so add them: 15/56 + 15/56 = 30/56 = 15/28 ≈ 0.536

4.3.3 Independent events LO 9

🍜 Destination: George Town, Penang

Formula

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)

Two events are independent when one does not change the probability of the other.

🎡 Interactive: plan a Penang food trip

You spin a wheel with 4 equal parts to choose dinner (Char Kway Teow, Assam Laksa, Nasi Kandar or Cendol), then flip a coin to choose how to get there (Trishaw or Walk). The coin doesn't know what the wheel said, so the events are independent.

Quick check: what is P(noodles AND walk)?
Noodles = Char Kway Teow or Assam Laksa, so P(noodles) = 2/4 = 1/2 P(walk) = 1/2 P(noodles and walk) = 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4 (2 of the 8 boxes in the grid ✓)

4.3.4 Mutually exclusive events LO 9

🏙️ Destination: Kuala Lumpur

Formula

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)

Two events are mutually exclusive when they cannot happen at the same time: they have no outcomes in common, so P(A and B) = 0.

🃏 Interactive: the KL day-trip lucky draw

A travel fair gives away a free day trip. You draw ONE card from 20: 8 Petronas Twin Towers 🏙️, 5 Batu Caves 🛕, 4 KL Tower 🗼 and 3 Aquaria KLCC 🐠. Pick two events and check whether they are mutually exclusive.

in A only in B only in both A and B

Quick check: what is P(Batu Caves or KL Tower)?
One card can't be both, so the events are mutually exclusive. P(Batu Caves or KL Tower) = 5/20 + 4/20 = 9/20 = 0.45

4.3.5 Non-mutually exclusive events LO 9

🐢 Destination: Sabah

Formula

P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)

Two events are non-mutually exclusive when they can happen at the same time. The outcomes in both get counted twice when you add, so subtract them once.

🤿 Interactive: the Sabah tour group

A tour group of 40 travellers visits Sabah. 18 went scuba diving at Sipadan (D) and 15 climbed Mount Kinabalu (K). Some did both. One traveller is chosen at random for a prize. Move the slider to change how many did both.

Quick check (7 did both): what is P(the winner did only ONE of the two activities)?
Diving only = 18 − 7 = 11, climbing only = 15 − 7 = 8 P(only one) = (11 + 8)/40 = 19/40 = 0.475
